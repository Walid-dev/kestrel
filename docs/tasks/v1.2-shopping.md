# v1.2 · Shopping — the plan

**Status: GO given 2026-11-25.** Two builders build it: the engine first, the pages second. The engine is built (E1 to
E8: the store and list engine, the evals, the shared reading parts, the store-page reader, Understand and Compare, the
offers and the deal, the run with Live and Ask, the watch and its pings). The pages: P1 is built (Shopping in the
app map, violet, and its page); P2 to P4 come next.

## Why

I often need to buy something, and I don't have time to check which products are best or where the price is good.
The big assistants do this badly where it matters most:

- **Prices.** A wrong price is their most common error. When a store hides its price, a chatbot guesses one.
- **Picks.** On a shopping benchmark, the best models picked a product that met every requirement less than one time
  in five. They find many products but choose poorly.
- **Discounts.** Most sale prices were matched or beaten at another time of the year.
- **My region.** Price history, price tracking and comparison tools mostly cover one country, and one store at most
  in mine.

kestrel can do better with less. It reads the sources that test products, checks every claim against its source,
reads prices from the stores' own page data, keeps its own price history, and says what it couldn't read.

## What it is

Shopping is a list of my wants. I write one in plain words ("a quiet blender for smoothies"), with a budget
or a note if I like. Each want runs on its own, step by step, shown in Live and on its card:

1. **Understand.** kestrel names the kind of product, what matters for it, and what my words already say. It asks
   up to three quick questions, only when the answer would change the pick. It never waits for my answers: it goes
   on with a default for each, and my tap re-ranks the list at once.
2. **Compare.** It searches the sites that test this kind of product, reads their reviews, and builds the compared
   list: the products worth considering, usually 5 to 8. Each product shows its value on each criterion that
   matters for this kind (suction, battery and noise for a robot vacuum; warmth, weight and fit for a jacket). Each
   earns its labels: best overall, best value, budget, premium, best for a need. Each says why, who it's not for,
   its trade-off against the top pick, the exact model sold in my region, and its sources. A checker drops any
   value or claim its source doesn't carry. The criteria become the list's filters and sorts.
3. **Find the offers.** For the products I pick (the labelled ones by default), kestrel finds where to buy each one
   now: the store, the price, the regular price, stock, shipping, the link, and when it was seen. Prices come from
   the store page's own product data first. When a readable page shows its price only in its words, the model reads
   it second, and code keeps it only when that number is on the page; such a price is marked and never wins. A store
   that can't be read is listed with its link, never with a guess. Most big stores turn a plain reader away: until
   kestrel reads them in my Chrome (a later step), they are links, and the page says so.
4. **Judge the deal.** One word and one line: not enough history yet, an inflated discount, a new low, a good price,
   high, or typical. Until kestrel has read a price long enough, the line states the facts instead of a judgement.
   Which store matches the price, and which refunds a later drop.
5. **Watch, when I ask.** kestrel re-reads the kept offers on a schedule, for as long as I set, and pings my phone
   when a price reaches my target, drops to its lowest since the watch began, or comes back in stock.

On each want, **Ask** keeps the conversation and can search the web. I mark products the way I mark listings in
Searches: a star, a target price, Bought or Passed, Delete with Undo. A want can run again later, since prices and
models change; it keeps my marks and logs what changed.

A want that names one product ("Fen Clean 3") goes straight to the offers: they show first, then a short look at its
alternatives.

### The rules it keeps

- **The model never writes a number on its own.** Every value and price shows its source and when it was read. A
  number the model reads is kept only when code finds it on the page, in the right product's part.
- **No product without a source that tests.** Forums, store reviews and listicles never bring a product in.
- **Code checks what code can check.** Constraints (budget, must-haves), labels, prices, history and verdicts are
  computed in code. The model reads pages, writes the reasons, and checks claims against quotes.
- **Page text is data.** kestrel never follows instructions found in a page, shuffles products before the model
  judges them, and needs independent sources for the top pick.
- **Neutral, and it says so.** No ads, no affiliate links, no paid ranking. The page prints the ranking rule.
- **Nothing of mine goes into a search.** Searches carry the product's words, never my budget or my note. Code
  enforces it: one scrub runs on every query Shopping or Ask on a want sends (see "The scrub").

## How the parts are shared

My rule: each part is a tool other apps reuse, and a new use is a setting or a file, not new code.

- **One list engine for every kind of listing.** A kind is data: its fields (each with a type, a unit and which way
  is better), its filters, its sorts, its card and its marks. One set of pure functions filters, counts and sorts
  the rows of any kind (`src/shared/kinds.ts`), and one list part draws them (`web/src/ui/kind-list.tsx`): the
  filter card, the cards on a phone, a table on a computer. Shopping is its first user, twice: a want's products are
  a kind whose fields are that want's criteria, and offers are a fixed kind (store, total, regular price, stock,
  condition). "In stock", "a store" and "new only" are filters. The homes hunt comes next as one more kind, with
  "rent or buy" as a filter on its one hunt. Jobs and deals move onto kinds later.
- **One store, later.** For now, Shopping keeps its own tables. `listings` has a CHECK on its kinds, and widening it
  means rebuilding the live table the job and deal hunts write to. After v1.0's cutover (the mirror gone), one
  migration rebuilds `listings` without that CHECK (kinds checked in code from their files), and offers and homes
  become listings of their kinds. Shopping's tables are shaped so that move is a copy.
- **Research's engine.** The page gathering in `src/domain/research.ts` (search several queries, merge the hits,
  open more pages than needed and keep the first that read well) moves into one function both use. The read tools
  (`src/domain/ask/tools.ts`) serve Ask on a want as they serve Ask elsewhere.
- **News' reader.** News fetches public pages itself (an honest user agent, the public-web guard on every hop, a
  size cap, a time limit). That fetch moves into `src/ai/web.ts`, and Shopping's readers use it, for store pages and
  review pages alike. The door's page reader can't serve them well: it turns a page into text, drops the scripts
  where the product data lives, and keeps 8,000 characters from the head, while a best-of page puts its comparison
  table and later picks further down. A small HTML-to-text step (`pageText`, built on News' `asText`) keeps tables
  as rows; the door's reader stays the fallback when kestrel can't read a page itself.
- **News' number check.** Every number a sentence writes must be in its sources: News does this in code
  (`numbersIn`). The function moves to one shared place, and Shopping's checker uses it.
- **The data files.** What changes by product or by country is data, not code: one file per category of product,
  one file of source tiers, one file per region (its stores, their policies, its model-code rules). A new category
  or a new region is a new file.

## The data files

### A category file (`src/domain/shopping/categories/<key>.json`)

One per kind of product. Six ship as starters, written from how the testing outlets test them: TV, headphones,
laptop, robot vacuum, winter jacket, winter boots. Any other kind is drafted by Understand at the want's first run
and saved to the data folder (`shopping/categories/<key>.json`, `draft: true`). The key comes from the model, so code
guards it: zod requires `^[a-z0-9-]{2,40}$`, the joined path must stay inside the categories folder, and a draft
never overrides a starter (a draft whose key a starter has gets a suffix: `tv-2`). A key that fails is refused and the
draft takes one made from its name (`../settings` for "Bike phone mount" becomes `bike-phone-mount`). A file I write there by hand,
without `draft`, may override a starter. A draft lists six products (`listSize` 6) unless it says otherwise.

```json
{
  "key": "robot-vacuum",
  "name": "Robot vacuum",
  "words": ["robot vacuum", "robovac"],
  "refreshMonths": 9,
  "listSize": 8,
  "criteria": [
    {
      "key": "pickup-carpet",
      "label": "Carpet pickup",
      "type": "score",
      "better": "higher",
      "weight": 25,
      "how": "share of embedded hair picked up from low-pile carpet"
    },
    {
      "key": "noise",
      "label": "Noise",
      "type": "number",
      "unit": "dB",
      "measured": true,
      "better": "lower",
      "weight": 10
    }
  ],
  "uses": { "carpet": { "pickup-carpet": 35, "tangle": 15 } },
  "implied": [{ "when": "long hair", "needs": ["a tangle-resistant brushroll"] }],
  "questions": [
    {
      "key": "floors",
      "q": "Mostly carpet or hard floors?",
      "options": [
        { "label": "Mostly carpet", "weights": { "pickup-carpet": 35 } },
        { "label": "Mostly hard floors", "weights": { "pickup-hard": 35 } },
        { "label": "A mix", "weights": {} }
      ],
      "default": 2
    }
  ],
  "traps": ["the same line sold under a value brand is not the same product"],
  "tags": ["mains-powered"],
  "sources": ["rtings.com", "vacuumwars.com"],
  "storeGroups": ["electronics", "home"],
  "draft": false
}
```

- `criteria`: four to seven, the weights summing to 100. `type` is `number` (with a unit), `score` (a source's own
  scale), `bool` or `enum`. A `score`, and a `number` marked `measured` (a test result such as noise in dB, which each
  lab measures its own way), is compared only within one source. An unmarked `number` is a stated spec (a weight, a
  screen size) that sources share.
- `uses` and a question's options change weights; an option may also add a must-have (`"hard": [...]`). The weights
  they name replace those criteria's weights; then all weights are scaled back to a sum of 100.
- `tags` say what kind of rules apply (`mains-powered`, `child-seat`, `sunscreen`). The region file holds the rule for
  each tag (the safety mark to confirm), so a new region needs no category edited. The want's page shows the rules
  in a "Before you buy" box.
- `sources` name the hosts that test this kind; the source file says how far each is trusted.

### The source file (`src/domain/shopping/sources.json`)

Each host with its tier and how it tests: A buys its own units and publishes its method and numbers; B tests hands-on
without numbers; C is a signal only (store reviews, forums); `never` is never evidence (paid seals, pages with no
method). A host the file doesn't list counts as B when its page states a testing method (it buys its units, it
publishes measurements), else C: a drafted category has no listed testers, and a listicle still never brings a product. `family` groups sister sites, which count once. `access` says how it reads: `plain`, `paywalled` (snippets
only) or `blocked` (never fetched). The starters come from the research: the tier-A labs, the hands-on outlets, the
forums that turn automated readers away (snippets only), the paid seals.

### The region file (`src/domain/shopping/regions/<key>.json`)

The setting `shopping.region` names it; when only one region file ships, it is the default. The first is Canada's.

- `currency`, `name`, and `searchWords` added to a region search.
- `stores`: each with its domain, its category groups, `read` (`page`, or `link` for a store kestrel only links to),
  a `searchUrl` with `{q}` for a link to its own search, `matches` (who it matches, the percentage it beats, the
  days) and `protects` (the days it refunds its own drop), each policy with the date it was checked. The first file
  ships with none: the research recorded which stores read, not their policies, and a policy never ships unchecked; the
  verdict's policies line stays empty until one is added with its date. The research
  found most big stores block a plain reader, and some others give no price; those ship as `read: "link"`, with the
  date of the test. A link store costs no search and no fetch: its search link shows at once.
- `brandStores`: a brand's own store, by brand (`{"Brisk": "brisk.example"}`). Only a listed brand store is read. An
  open search could return a fake or dead shop with clean product data, so kestrel never reads a store it found by
  searching.
- `tagRules`: the rule for each category tag (`mains-powered`: a recognised safety mark on the product itself).
- `linkTools`: price-history pages kestrel links to and never reads (an Amazon price chart by ASIN, a PC-parts
  comparison by words).
- `modelRules`: a brand's code for this region against another region's (a TV code ending ZA elsewhere and ZC here).
- `reviewSources`: the region's own testers, added to the source file's.

Amazon's site, the main deal forum, the price-history sites and flyer sites are `link` or `linkTools` only: their
rules bar automated readers, they charge AI readers, or they need my postal code.

### Settings (`settings.json` in the data folder)

`shopping: { region, read }`. `read` lets kestrel fetch store pages itself: on in the deployed app, off in a made-up
folder unless its settings name it, as News. Pings use `alerts.topics.shopping`, or the deals channel when it is
unset.

## The technical design

### The tables (migration 030, renumbered at merge)

`src/db/migrate.ts` applies every file numbered above `PRAGMA user_version`. If another branch's 030 reaches a
database first, Shopping's 030 is skipped in silence and every route fails. So: the migration takes the next free
number when it merges (an applied migration is never renumbered). `src/db/db.test.ts` already fails when two migration
files share a number or one is missing, and says the fix ("give the newer one the next free number").

- `wants`: `id`, `words`, `budget` (whole units of the region's currency, or NULL), `note`, `state` (`queued`,
  `running`, `done`, `failed`), `job` (what the queued or running work is: `{"do": "full"}`, or `{"do": "offers",
  "products": [ids]}` for Find offers on products I picked), `step`, `status` (`open`, `bought`, `passed`), `starred`,
  `need` (the need card as
  JSON, with its category's criteria, questions, uses and list size taken along, so a category file edited later or a
  draft replaced never changes a want's list), `answers` (my picks, `{"<question key>": <option index>}`), `result` (sources, assumptions, what would
  change the pick, conflicts, the "before you buy" checks, as JSON), `log` (the dated changes, JSON), `error`,
  `created`, `updated`, `ran`.
- `products`: `id`, `want`, `key` (brand and model, normalised: a re-run finds the product by it), `name`, `brand`,
  `models` (every code it is sold under, the region's first), `facts` (`{criterion: {v, unit, ref, quote, unsure,
  others}}`: the value used, from one source; `unsure` when its quote isn't in the product's own part of the page;
  `others` the other sources' values, as notes),
  `labels`, `score`, `place`, `out` (why it is only "also considered"; empty for a finalist; a product a run no longer
  finds stays, with my marks and its history, as "no longer in any source"), `flags` (its must-haves no source states),
  `words` (why, the
  trade-off, who it's not for: checked sentences with their refs), `usual` (a usual price in the region's currency,
  in cents, when a source states one), `evidence` (sources, A and B counts, the newest date, thin, old), `starred`,
  `status`, `target` (cents), `watch` (`{until, everyHours, stock, last}`), `mine`, `found`, `updated`.
  `UNIQUE (want, key)`.
- `offers`: `id`, `product`, `store`, `url`, `seller`, `condition` (`new`, `open-box`, `refurbished`, `used`,
  `marketplace`), `price`, `regular`, `shipping` (cents), `currency`, `stock` (`in`, `out`, `preorder`, `unknown`),
  `pickup`, `valid_until`, `method` (`shopify`, `jsonld`, `meta`, `microdata`, `text`, `link`, or empty when none
  was read), `read` (`ok`, `blocked`, `unreadable`, `link`, `mismatch`, `unsearched`), `words` (for a `text` price, the
  page's exact words), `why` (a row with no price: why, in words), `seen`. `UNIQUE (product, url)`.
- `price_readings`: `offer`, `at`, `price`, `regular`, `stock`, `method`. Every reading kestrel makes, from the first
  one: the history no other tool keeps across stores in my region.
- `shop_deleted`: a deleted want or product, whole (its row and its children as JSON), with `what`, its `want`, its
  `key` and `name`, and when. A re-run never brings a deleted product back (its want and key); Restore puts it back as
  it was. A want deleted while queued comes back failed, its job gone (Run again starts it).

Money is in cents, as integers. Only `src/domain/shopping/` writes these tables.

### The shared shapes (`src/shared/shopping.ts`, `src/shared/kinds.ts`)

Plain TypeScript, no zod (the web reads them). `kinds.ts`: `Field`, `ListKind`, `Row`, and `filterRows`,
`pillCounts` (each pill's count under the other filters), `sortRows`, `numberSteps` (two or three "at least" steps
from a column's values). `shopping.ts`: `WantSummary`, `WantView` (the need, the want's `ListKind`, the questions
with my answers, the products, their offers, verdicts and evidence, the sources, the log, the state and step),
`OfferView`, `Verdict`, `Watch`, and `OFFER_KIND`. These shapes are the contract: the engine fills them, the pages
draw them.

### Understand (one call, `shopping` use)

The prompt (`src/ai/shopping/prompt.ts`, `understandPrompt`) carries: the want's words; the budget as a number; the
note; the known categories (key and words); for a matched category, its criteria, uses, implied needs and questions.
It asks for:

- the category: a known key, or a new one with four to seven criteria (key, label, type, unit, better, weight, how
  it is measured);
- the mode: `compare`, or `one` with the product it names;
- the use (who and what for, from the words), the hard constraints the words state (budget, size, compatibility,
  must-haves, each tied to a criterion when one fits), the implied needs;
- the questions: none to three, only those whose answer changes the top pick, never one the words already answer,
  each with two to four options, each option's effect on weights or must-haves, and its default;
- `searchWords`: two to six plain words naming the product and the use. Never the budget, the note, a person or a
  place.

```json
{
  "mode": "compare",
  "category": "robot-vacuum",
  "use": "carpet",
  "newCategory": null,
  "product": "",
  "hard": [{ "criterion": "", "says": "under the budget" }],
  "implied": ["a tangle-resistant brushroll"],
  "questions": [
    { "key": "floors", "q": "...", "options": [{ "label": "...", "weights": {}, "hard": [] }], "default": 2 }
  ],
  "searchWords": "robot vacuum carpet"
}
```

The code reads it tolerantly (zod, `src/ai/shopping/shape.ts`), drops a question or an effect that names a criterion
the category lacks, keeps three questions at most, and runs the scrub on `searchWords`. A new category is saved as a
draft category file (its key guarded as above). My answers later change weights and must-haves only, so an answer
re-ranks in code at once and never needs a new run.

### The scrub (`src/domain/shopping/scrub.ts`, pure)

The prompt asks the model to keep my budget and note out of a search. A local model can still write "under 600", and
a budget often sits inside the want's own words ("a robot vacuum for pet hair under $600"). So code removes them from
every query, whatever the prompt says:

- every money amount and its words: a currency sign or code with a number, "under N", "below N", "max N", "N
  dollars", "$Nk", and the word "budget". "Under N" with a unit after it ("under 2 kg") is a size and stays, and a bare
  "4K" is a screen, not a price;
- every number the need card's hard constraints hold as a budget, in any form ("600", "600.00", "$600");
- every word of my note, except a word that is also in the category's words or the want's product words (the want's
  words with the money phrases taken out), so a note that repeats "carpet" doesn't empty the query.

`scrubQuery(query, ctx)` returns the cleaned query, or nothing when no product word is left (the search is then
skipped); `scrubContext(want, need, products)` builds what it needs once. A product's own name or code in a query is
masked first, so "Fen 300" keeps its 300 whatever my budget is. A budget my words state ("under $150") is also my
budget for the ranking's gate when I set none. It runs on every query Shopping builds: Compare's searches, one mode's searches and the
offers' searches. It also runs inside Ask on a want (see "Ask on a want").

### The room for each call

Each call starts with a set room and a set page budget; the live eval tunes them, and a call that runs out of room
writes one line in the run's log ("the reader ran out of room on reviews.example") rather than failing the run.

| Call              | maxTokens | thinking | page text                                  |
| ----------------- | --------- | -------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| Understand        | 4,000     | on       | none                                       |
| Read a page       | 6,000     | off      | 20,000 characters                          |
| Read a text price | 1,200     | off      | 6,000 characters around the product's name |
| Write             | 8,000     | on       | none                                       |
| Check             | 3,000     | off      | none                                       |

### Compare

1. **Plan the searches, in code.** One search per trusted source for the category (four at most):
   `site:<host> best <searchWords>`. One open search: `best <searchWords> <year>`. One region search with the
   region's review sources and `searchWords`. Each query goes through the scrub. Rejected: a model planning the
   queries (the category's sources give better ones and save a call).
2. **Search and read** with the shared gathering (Research's): about three results a search, one retry built in.
   A run sends 24 searches at most, all steps together, one at a time, two seconds apart; the search tool drops
   requests sent faster. Hits are ranked by tier (A, then B, then the rest); `never` hosts are dropped; `blocked` and
   `paywalled` hosts give their snippets only. Eight pages at most are read, each through kestrel's own fetch and
   `pageText` (tables kept as rows), and through the door's reader when kestrel can't read it. A page longer than
   20,000 characters is cut by whole sections, never inside a table: sections that hold a table or a product code
   come first, then the rest in page order. The text the model sees is kept with the reading, so the quote check
   runs on exactly that text.
3. **Read each page** (one call a page, `readPrompt`). The page is data: the prompt says so and fences it. It asks for
   the products the page itself tested or recommends for this category (not ads, not "related"), each with: the
   page's own label, the model codes as written, a value for each listed criterion only when the page states it,
   each with the exact words copied from the page (25 words at most); the pros, the cons and who should skip it, each
   with its exact words; a price only if the page states one, with its currency; and the page's method (does it say
   it buys its own units, does it publish measurements, its date).

   ```json
   {"method": {"buysOwn": true, "numbers": true, "date": "2026-10"},
    "products": [{"name": "Brisk R7", "brand": "Brisk", "models": ["R7-C"], "label": "best overall",
      "facts": {"noise": {"v": 64, "unit": "dB", "quote": "measured at 64 dB on max"}},
      "pros": [{"text": "...", "quote": "..."}], "cons": [], "skip": [], "price": null}]}
   ```

   Code then checks each value in two ways, on folded text (Unicode quotes and dashes made plain, the `|`, `*` and
   `#` marks removed, case and runs of spaces ignored):
   - the quote is in the page text the model saw, and the number is in the quote; otherwise the value is dropped;
   - the quote sits in the product's own part of the page: after the product's name first appears and before the
     next product's name, or in the product's own table row. A small model often takes the next row's value, and a
     quote such as "64 dB" is on the page whatever product it belongs to. A value outside its product's part is
     kept as `unsure`: shown in grey with a "?" and never used to rank.

   Code also takes out of the page, before the model reads it, any line that talks to an assistant or tells its reader
   to drop its instructions; drops a product the page never names; keeps a page's label only when it sits in the
   product's part or on a line with its name; and keeps nothing from a page that holds none of the kind's words. Pros,
   cons and who should skip it are kept in the page's own words, in the product's part. Everything dropped is gone before
   the next step sees it.
4. **Merge, in code.** Codes are normalised (case, spaces, dashes, colour suffixes, the region's model rules), and
   records with a shared code, or the same brand and normalised name, are one product. Independent sources are
   counted (a family once). A product with no A or B source is dropped. For each criterion, one source gives the
   value: the source that covers the most products on it, ties broken by the category's `sources` order. The other
   sources' values stay as notes, and a difference over 10% goes to the conflicts. A spec criterion (an unmarked
   `number`) takes any source's value when the chosen source lacks it.
5. **Rank, in code** (`src/domain/shopping/rank.ts`, pure: the want, my answers, the products, their offers). All
   merged products are scored, then gated (the hard constraints); the finalists are the top scores that pass, up to
   the category's `listSize`, with coverage breaking ties. A mediocre product with many reviews can't push out a top
   product with one. The rest are "also considered", each with its reason. Each number criterion is scaled across
   the products by which way is better; a `score` or `measured` criterion uses only its one source's values, so
   scales never mix. A missing or `unsure` value gives its weight to the known ones, and coverage under half bars the
   top label. Weights come from the category, its use and my answers (replaced, then scaled back to 100). Hard
   constraints are checked one by one for each product, and the
   check is kept: a product that fails one goes to "also considered" with the reason ("over the budget", "no mop");
   an unknown is a flag, never a pass. The price is the lowest verified offer total, else a usual price in the
   region's currency, else none. Labels:
   - **Best overall:** the top score that passes every constraint, with two or more independent A or B sources (one
     source: "thin evidence").
   - **Best value:** the best score per dollar among the finalists within 10% of the top score; when none of them is
     cheaper than the best overall, among those priced 20% or more under the finalists' median. Either way, only
     above the quality floor (60% of the top score). It waits for a price.
   - **Budget:** the cheapest above a quality floor (60% of the top score); none when nothing clears it.
   - **Premium:** clearly better on a decisive criterion, for more money.
   - **Best for <need>:** the top on the criterion an implied need or an answer names.
   - Two labels at most per product, and none for a product with under half its weight known (a perfect score on one
     criterion is no reason to name it best value).

   Sources older than 12 months or than the category's refresh are flagged on the product.
6. **Write** (one call, `writePrompt`). The finalists in shuffled order, each with the labels the code gave (the model
   may not change them), its facts and quotes numbered `[q12]`, its evidence; the need card and my answers. Code writes
   the assumptions for the questions I haven't answered ("Taken as "A mix" until I answer"); the model's are checked as
   any sentence. For each:
   two or three reasons tied to the criteria and their numbers, its trade-off against the best overall, and who it's
   not for. For the list: the assumptions taken (the defaults), what would change the pick, the conflicts between
   sources. Every sentence ends with its refs; numbers only as the quotes give them; the plain style.

   ```json
   {"products": [{"id": "p3", "why": [{"text": "...", "refs": ["q12"]}],
     "tradeoff": {"text": "...", "refs": ["q4"]}, "notFor": {"text": "...", "refs": ["q9"]}}],
    "assumptions": [{"text": "...", "refs": []}], "changeIf": [], "conflicts": []}
   ```

7. **Check.** Code first: a ref must exist, and every number in a sentence must be in its refs (the shared
   `numbersIn`); a sentence that fails is dropped. Before the number check, code strips every finalist's name, brand
   and model codes (normalised) from the sentence: "The Fen 300 picks up more hair than the Orla S20" would otherwise
   need 300 and 20 in its quotes, and most correct reasons would vanish. Then one call (`checkPrompt`, the `shopping-check` use): each
   sentence with its quotes, numbered, answered supported or not ("supported" means the quotes say it, not that it is
   likely). Unsupported sentences are dropped. If that call fails, the code-checked sentences stay and the page says
   the second check didn't run.

A want in `one` mode goes to the offers first: it pins the named product (its codes from the first readable store
page), finds and shows its offers, keeps it first in its list whatever its score, and only then searches `"<product> review"`, `"<product> vs"` and one region
search (each scrubbed), reads three pages, and adds up to three alternatives the pages compare it with.

### Find the offers (`src/domain/shopping/offers.ts`, `src/ai/shopping/product-data.ts`)

For each picked product (the labelled ones by default, four at most):

1. **Pin it:** its codes and aliases, and its GTIN when a page gives one.
2. **The stores:** the region's stores in the category's groups (eight at most), plus the brand's own store when
   `brandStores` lists it. A `link` store gets its search link at once (the product's model in `{q}`), with no search
   and no fetch.
3. **Find the page,** for the `page` stores only: one search a store, `"<model>" site:<domain>`, then
   `"<brand> <model>"` once, each scrubbed and inside the run's 24 searches. A store left over when the searches run
   out is `unsearched`, with its search link. A store the searches found no page at keeps its search link too (`link`,
   "a search found no page for it"). A hit is kept only when its title or address holds one of the product's codes. A
   search snippet's price is never used. With `shopping.read` off, every store is a link at once and nothing is
   searched.
4. **Read it in code**, through the shared public fetch (an honest user agent, never a disguised one). The product
   page comes first, always; then these methods in order, the first that gives a price wins:
   1. **Shopify,** only when the page shows it is a Shopify store (the platform's own markers in the HTML, no site
      names). Then `/products/<handle>.js`, the handle taken from the path (also from
      `/collections/<x>/products/<handle>`). Its prices are whole cents (12900 is 129.00), so they are divided by
      100. It carries no currency: the currency comes from the page's JSON-LD or `og:price:currency`, else
      `/cart.js`, and the price is refused when none says the region's currency. The variant is the one in
      `?variant=`, else the one whose SKU or barcode holds the product's code. It gives the compare-at price (the
      regular one), `available` and the barcode.
   2. **JSON-LD** (`Product`, `Offer`, `AggregateOffer`, `ProductGroup` with its variants): price, currency,
      availability, valid until, seller, condition, a strikethrough price as the regular one.
   3. **The price meta tags,** then **microdata**.
   4. **The page's words,** last, for a readable page that matches the product and holds no structured price. One
      call (`textPricePrompt`, the `shopping` use) gets the fenced text around the product's name and returns the
      price, the regular price and the exact words for each. Code keeps a number only when it appears in the raw page
      within 300 characters of the product's code or name, and in the region's currency. Such an offer is `text`,
      shows "read from the page's words" with those words, never wins Best price, never pings, and its readings never
      make a verdict. Rejected: the model's price without that check (the invented prices the research measured).

   A page whose codes, GTIN or name don't hold one of the product's codes is a `mismatch`, shown apart and never
   ranked. A price in another currency is refused. A 403, a 429, a challenge page ("Just a moment", "Access Denied",
   "press and hold": generic markers, no site names) is `blocked`: the row says so, with the link.
5. **Be polite:** product pages only, never a store's search pages; robots.txt read once a day per host and obeyed
   (its Disallow and Crawl-delay); one request per host at a time, five seconds apart at least; a host that answers
   403 or 429 rests a day.
6. **Keep:** each offer is upserted by its address, and every reading goes into `price_readings`. The total is the
   price plus shipping when known. Rows sort by total, new and sold by the store first; open-box, refurbished and
   marketplace offers are rows of their own. "Best price" goes only to the lowest verified total (read from the
   page's data, not its words), new, sold by the store, in stock online. In stock means sold online, not on a shelf
   near me.

The page shows the read prices first, then one compact "Also check:" row with the link stores' search links, and a
line that says why: "These stores turn automated readers away; a later version reads them in the agent's Chrome."

Then `rank.ts` runs again, now with prices: best value can be named.

### Judge the deal (`src/domain/shopping/verdict.ts`, pure)

From the product's readings across stores (data readings only, never `text` ones), the offer's regular price, the
want's target and the region's policies. The first word that applies wins, in this order:

1. **Not enough history:** under 30 days of readings. The line still states the facts, never a judgement: "Lowest in
   the 9 days read, 18% under its regular price", "The 'was' price hasn't been seen in the 9 days read". It links
   the outside history where one exists (the Amazon chart, the PC-parts site).
2. **Inflated discount,** only on firm ground: kestrel's readings of this offer cover at least 150 of the last 180
   days and the "was" price shows in under half of them; or, over 30 days or more, another readable store's verified
   new price stayed under this "was" price on every reading. With less, the line states the fact ("The 'was' price
   hasn't been seen in the 41 days kestrel has read") and the word is never used. A store running a month-long sale
   must not be called a fake discount on day 30. The line adds "still the lowest kestrel has read" when that holds.
3. **New low:** under every reading of this product, in every store, by 1% at least.
4. **Good:** at or under the lowest of the last 90 days when that low sits under the 90-day median, or 10% or more
   under the median. A price that hasn't moved is typical, not good: at its low every day, it is no deal.
5. **High:** 10% or more over the 90-day median.
6. **Typical:** within 10% of the 90-day median.

The rule is kestrel's own, shaped after the Competition Bureau's regular-price tests (more than half the sales, or a
substantial period, usually within a year). It is not the law, and the page never says a store broke it. Readings
are not sales.

The line adds the policies: who matches the price, who refunds a drop and for how long. The words come from code
templates, never from the model. The price pill on a card and the verdict chip take their tone from one map (see
the chips below), so a price never shows two tones.

### The run (`src/domain/shopping/run.ts`)

- One want runs at a time, as the model works one request at a time. A new want while one runs is `queued`, with its
  `job`. When a run ends, it starts the next queued want itself (the oldest first), so a queued want never waits for
  the scheduler's tick behind a round of hunts. The tick's hook only catches up after a restart. While an
  application is at work, a run waits (it needs the model); price checks don't.
- A `full` job's steps: Understanding → Searching (n searches) → Reading reviews (k of n) → Comparing → Writing →
  Checking → Finding offers (store k of n) → Done. An `offers` job (Find offers on products I picked) runs only
  Finding offers, for its products, then re-ranks. Each step is written to the want's row and to Live
  (`begin("shopping", …)`, a new Live kind), linked to the want's page.
- A failure ends the run with its words (`failureWords`, "Run again") and keeps what the last good run found.
- A restart marks a running want failed: "kestrel restarted while it ran: Run again". A queued want stays queued,
  with its job; Shopping's mount starts it when the model is free, and the tick's hook catches up after that.
- Run again keeps my marks (star, target, status, watch) on products found again by key, and logs what changed:
  "Fen 3 dropped: no longer in any source", "Orla S2 added", "Brisk R7: $499 → $449". It keeps the need card too:
  Understand runs once, so my answers keep their meaning; a want I'd word differently is a new want.
- Every call is recorded (`recordCall`): `shopping-understand:v1`, `shopping-read:v1`, `shopping-write:v1`,
  `shopping-check:v1`.

### The models (`src/domain/models.ts`)

Two uses on the Models page, grouped under Shopping: `shopping` (Understand, the readers, the writer; the careful
model by default) and `shopping-check` (the checker; it follows `shopping` unless picked apart, since the two models
don't fit in memory together and a swap costs time). The live eval run picks the defaults and whether the readers
think.

### Watch and pings (`src/domain/shopping/watch.ts`)

- A watch is on one product: until a date (one week, two weeks, one month or three months; two weeks by default),
  every 4 h, 12 h or a day (a day by default), and whether a return to stock pings.
- The scheduler's tick gains a Shopping hook, next to News' and before the searches, in its own try/catch (a failure
  is one log line and never stops the tick). It returns at once: it starts a queued want left by a restart and the
  due price checks, in the background. A check re-reads the product's offers that read well from the page's data or
  rested long enough; `link` and `text` rows are never re-read (no model in a check). If a page no longer matches,
  it becomes a `mismatch`.
- A ping when, after the read, the best verified total is at or under my target (once per crossing: it pings again
  only after the price went above and came back, or fell 1% more), or is a **price drop**, the lowest since the watch
  began by 1% and a dollar at least, or the product came back in stock. "New low" stays the verdict's word, so the
  ping and the sheet never disagree in a watch's first weeks. One ping per want per check: its title names the best
  ("Brisk R7 $449 at Store"), its lines the target, the verdict and the regular price, and a tap opens the product on
  the want's page.
- The channel: `alerts.topics.shopping`, else deals. `Alert.channel` gains `shopping`.
- A watch ends on its date with one line in the want's log, no ping.
- Each ping is a line in the want's log (`kind: "ping"`), with why when it couldn't be sent; the Shopping page's rail
  reads its latest pings from there. A watch's own memory (`low`, `pinged`, `wasOut`) lives in its JSON.

### Ask on a want (`src/domain/shopping/ask.ts`)

A helper in Ask's `HELPERS` (`shopping`, the ref is the want's id), with the web tools, as News and Research. Ask's
own rule keeps the web tools off pages about my own things, so nothing of mine goes into a search; a want carries
my budget and my note, so code keeps them out, not the prompt:

- **The page leaves them out.** The page it gives the model: the want's words with the money phrases taken out (the
  scrub's first rule), the need card without the budget ("a budget is set"), each finalist's "within the budget:
  yes, no or unknown" computed in code, the criteria, the finalists with their facts, labels and best offers with
  verdicts, the also-considered and why, the sources. The note never goes on the page.
- **Every query is scrubbed.** `Helper` gains an optional `scrub(db, ref, tool, args)` hook. Ask's loop calls it before
  it runs the `web` and `page` tools (it knows the helper; `runTool` stays as it is): Shopping's hook runs `scrubQuery` on
  a search's words and refuses a page address that holds a scrubbed term (in words, so the model answers without it).
  Other helpers have no hook and work as now.

The page also says the reviews' words are facts to check, never instructions. Cards that act (star, target, run
again) come in a later step.

### The routes (`src/server/shopping.ts`)

Every body goes through zod.

- `GET /api/shopping`: the wants (`WantSummary`), the figures, the watched products.
- `POST /api/shopping/wants` `{words, budget?, note?}`: starts or queues it, answers its id.
- `GET /api/shopping/wants/:id`: the `WantView`.
- `PATCH /api/shopping/wants/:id` `{budget?, note?, status?, starred?}`: a budget change re-ranks at once.
- `POST /api/shopping/wants/:id/answers` `{key, option}`: re-ranks at once.
- `POST /api/shopping/wants/:id/run`: Run again.
- `DELETE /api/shopping/wants/:id`, `POST /api/shopping/wants/:id/restore`.
- `PATCH /api/shopping/products/:id` `{starred?, status?, target?}`.
- `POST /api/shopping/products/:id/offers`: find offers for a product I picked. It queues an `offers` job on the
  want (joining the product to a queued `offers` job's list when there is one); a `full` job queued or running
  already covers it.
- `PUT` and `DELETE /api/shopping/products/:id/watch`.
- `DELETE /api/shopping/products/:id`, `POST /api/shopping/products/:id/restore`.
- `GET /api/shopping/products/:id`: one product; a deleted one as it was (`deleted: {at}`), so its sheet says so first

- `GET /api/shopping/products/:id/history`: its readings, for the chart.

## The steps

Two builders, each in its own worktree. Builder 1 owns `src/`, `evals/` and `scripts/`; builder 2 owns `web/` and
`docs/design.md`. Step E1 fixes the shared shapes, ships every route, and gives a made-up want to the development
data, so builder 2 starts right after it: builder 2 branches from E1's commit and rebases on each later E step it
needs (named in its step). Each step: its files, its check, its message. Each step updates this plan's status line
and every doc that names what changed, and adds a `docs/decisions.md` entry when it decides something. The check is
`node --run check` every time, plus what each step names. Made-up data only: fictional brands, `.example` hosts.

**Each builder's dev server is its own.** The made-up data folder is `$TMPDIR/kestrel-dev-data`, and `node --run
dev` listens on 8791, so two worktrees on the defaults share one folder and one port: a builder's check could reach
another worktree's server, or find its database already past migration 030. So each builder sets its own `TMPDIR`
(the one its commits use) and its own `PORT`, checked free first (`lsof -i :<port>`): builder 1 on 8792, builder 2
on 8793, never 8790 (the real app's) or 8791. Vite's proxy is fixed at 8791 in `vite.config.ts`; in P1, builder 2
makes it read `KESTREL_API_PORT` (8791 when unset), a one-line change it owns for this version.

## Rejected

- **Offers inside `listings` now.** Widening its kind CHECK rebuilds the live table of the job and deal hunts.
- **A model-planned search list.** The category's testing sources give better queries for no call.
- **Prices from search snippets, review pages, or the model unchecked.** They are the stale and invented prices the
  research measured. Code reads the page's own data first; the model reads a page's words only as the last method,
  and code keeps its number only when it is on that page, by that product.
- **A prompt as the only guard on my budget and note.** A local model still writes them into a query; the scrub is
  code.
- **Reading a brand store an open search found.** Fake and dead shops show up in where-to-buy answers; only a listed
  brand store is read.
- **A data-folder override for the builders.** `TMPDIR` already gives each worktree its own made-up folder.
- **Waiting for my answers.** A want would sit until I come back. Defaults go on; an answer re-ranks in code.
- **A browser-like user agent** to pass a store's check. kestrel reads honestly or not at all.
- **Reading Amazon, the paid deal forum, price-history sites or flyer sites.** Their rules bar it, they charge AI
  readers, or they need my postal code. They are links.
- **A long list.** Choice gets harder past a handful; 5 to 8 finalists and an "also considered" fold.
- **Wide comparison tables on a phone.** Cards there; the table from a computer's width.
- **A robots.txt list per store.** One general reader of robots.txt serves every store.

## Facts behind it (researched 2026-11-25)

- Trust comes from method: the A-tier outlets buy their own units and publish their numbers. Reliability gates a pick
  (one consumer group withholds its top award for poor reliability).
- AI agents pick constraint-breaking products, take the first offer, and are moved by list position and planted page
  text (ShoppingComp, Magentic Marketplace, ACES).
- Of 21 stores in my region tested with a plain reader, four gave machine-readable prices (JSON-LD or Shopify JSON);
  eleven blocked it, and two more gave no price. The door's page reader drops the scripts that hold JSON-LD.
- Shopify's product `.js` gives prices in whole cents and no currency (its documentation).
- The regular-price tests in my region (the Competition Bureau's): more than half the sales at that price or higher,
  or offered in good faith at that price for a substantial period, usually within a year. Readings are not sales,
  so kestrel's own rule is stricter about when it says "inflated".
- About a third of last year's sale-day deals in my region had been cheaper earlier in the season.
- Regional model codes differ (ZA against ZC, one letter for a store's exclusive), and warranty can depend on them.
