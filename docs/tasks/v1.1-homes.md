# v1.1 · Homes — the plan

**Status: proposed (2026-11-25), waiting for my GO.** One homes hunt in Searches: condos and apartments to rent and to
buy, at the job and deal hunts' standard. Its build runs on made-up data. The real hunt runs on what kestrel owns,
after v1.0's own collectors. Nothing is built yet.

## Why

I want a condo search, to rent or to buy or both, at exactly the standard of the other search pages. Two rules shape
it:

- **Rent or buy is a filter, not a question.** One hunt collects both, and I pick To rent or To buy the way I pick a
  stage. Bedrooms, price, area, the site and who lists are filters too.
- **My details come later.** The areas, the budget and the must-haves go into the hunt's settings in my data folder
  when I have them. Until then the hunt collects, ranks what it can, and never pings. Nothing in this plan waits for
  them.

Searches already has the engine: searches that run on their own, one line of runs, listings, scores, Priority, the
sheet, deep checks, Ask and pings. Homes need four things that jobs and deals don't:

- **Money in two shapes:** a rent a month, or a price with condo fees a month.
- **A place:** the areas I name, matched without a map service.
- **One home on many sites:** one unit can show on six sites at once.
- **Scams:** rentals are their main target. One Canadian renter in three met a fake listing ([liv.rent's
  survey][liv-survey]); in US data, about half of rental-scam reports began on Facebook ([FTC, 2025][ftc-2025]).

## In short

1. Each search of the hunt reads one site: its alert emails first, if I let kestrel read their mailbox; its pages
   only where I say yes.
2. Each home lands once, whatever site found it. The other sites show as "+2".
3. Plain code scores it against my rules, reads its features and looks for scam signs.
4. The page is the job and deal hunts' page: the cards, the filters I pick (rent or buy among them), Priority, the
   sheet and Ask.
5. On my tap, a deep check says what to know: is it real, to rent; what the condo's papers say, to buy. A scam becomes
   Not real.
6. A great new home pings my phone, once a round.

## Everything the other hunts have, the same way

- **The hunt page:** the hunt's name as the switcher, On / Quiet / Paused, Search now, the settings, the status line
  and About.
- **The cards:** the Searches card, with the home's money as its figure (decision 3).
- **The filters:** the find box and Reset, then a row of pills with their counts for each filter, each row folding on
  its own (decision 4).
- **The sheet:** the facts, the stages, the star, my note, the deep check, the quiet Delete and Restore.
- **Priority:** every great match and every home I star, through Contacted and Visit booked, until I unstar it or set
  it aside.
- **Ask:** it finds through the hunt in words; a stage, a delete or a deep check on one tap; a change to many as one
  rule kestrel resolves, with Undo (decision 10).
- **Search now, Live and the line:** unchanged. Live says "Made-up board: 2 of 4, 12 homes so far".
- **Pings:** one per round for new great homes (decision 11).
- **My marks:** a stage, a star, a note or a delete is mine, as on every listing. No mirror reaches homes: the old
  setup has no homes hunt. A delete's tombstone keeps every site's copy out (decision 8).

## The decisions

### 1. One hunt; rent or buy is a filter

One homes hunt collects homes to rent and homes to buy. Each home carries its deal, `rent` or `buy`, from its site.
The page's "Rent or buy" row offers All, To rent and To buy.

- **The rules hold both deals.** The hunt's rules have a block for each: a rent a month to rent; a price and the condo
  fees to buy. Each home is judged by its own deal's block. A deal with no block yet is judged on everything but
  money.
- **The searches decide what comes in.** Each search reads one site, for one deal or both. To stop collecting sales, I
  switch the sale searches off. No setting asks rent or buy.
- **One hunt sees both markets.** An owner's rental whose unit an agent lists for sale in the same hunt is a scam
  sign (decision 7), with no second hunt to read.

Rejected: two hunts, one to rent and one to buy (an earlier draft): I would have to choose up front, and rent or buy
is a filter I pick. A hunt-level "rent or buy" setting, for the same reason. Mortgage maths to put a price in months:
its assumptions would need keeping current, and a rent-or-buy calculator is a feature of its own.

### 2. A third kind of listing, on the shared kind engine

Homes are listings of a third kind, beside jobs and deals, in the same `listings` table. Their fields, filters and
sorts are a kind on the engine the Shopping plan sets (v1.2, `docs/tasks/v1.2-shopping.md`, on its own branch until it
merges): `src/shared/kinds.ts` as its step E1 built it, its `ListKind` and its pure functions. This plan's number
came before Shopping's, but homes build after Shopping's E1.

- **One store.** The specs, the line, the collector door, the marks, the tombstones, Priority, the sheet, Ask and the
  deep check serve homes as they serve jobs and deals. Nothing is copied.
- **The kind as data.** `HOME_KIND` (`src/shared/homes.ts`) names each field (its type, its unit, which way is
  better), the filters, the sorts and the card's fields, as `OFFER_KIND` does for Shopping. The server and the page
  read the same file.
- **Kinds checked in code.** `listings.kind` has a CHECK for jobs and deals. SQLite's ALTER TABLE can't change a
  CHECK. So one migration rebuilds `listings` without it. The new table has the same columns in the same order, with
  their defaults. Every row, every index and the `UNIQUE (search, dedupe)` come across. The spec's zod shape and the
  collector door check the kind instead.
- **This rebuild comes with homes.** The Shopping plan placed it after v1.0's cutover, once the mirror is gone. Homes
  never use the mirror. Waiting for it to go would hold them back for weeks. The rebuild doesn't change what the
  mirror writes: the import names its columns, and they all stay. The migration runner copies the database before any
  migration runs. A test proves every row, mark and tombstone survives. Shopping's later move of offers into
  `listings` then needs no second rebuild. Shopping's text ("One store, later") changes when the branches merge, not
  before: its builders are still editing it.
- **Each two-way jobs-or-deals choice becomes a lookup by kind.** The nouns, the stage words, the set-aside word, the
  sorts, the eyebrow and its tone come from one map, `src/shared/hunt-kinds.ts`, which the server and the page both
  read. A fourth kind costs a row, not a branch.
- **The ten stages stay** (one set keeps every screen and filter shared, 2026-11-21), each in the job stage's colour.
  A home's words: To review, Interested, Not sure, Contacted (`applied`), Visit booked (`interviewing`), Applied to
  rent or Offer made to buy (`offer`, by the home's deal), Turned down (`rejected`), Gone, rented or sold (`closed`),
  Not real (`fake`), Not for me (`passed`).

Rejected: waiting for the cutover to drop the CHECK (homes would wait weeks on a bridge they never use); tables of
homes' own, as Shopping has (two of every mark, tombstone, Priority rule, Ask rule and deep check); a home stored as a
deal (a deal's identity is its link and its usual price is per model, so every deal function would grow a home
branch); widening the CHECK to three kinds (a fourth kind would rebuild the table again).

### 3. A home's fields, and its card

A homes collector prints the collector door's item with a `home` part, checked by its own zod shape. A field the site
doesn't give stays empty: nothing is guessed.

- **From the collector door's item:** the title, the link, the site, when it was posted, the site's place words
  (`location`), the money (`price`: the rent a month, or the asking price) and the listing's words (`excerpt`, 4,000
  characters at most). The words come from a page read only: an email gives fields, never its words.
- **The `home` part:**
  - the deal: `rent` or `buy`;
  - where: the address, the unit, the postal code;
  - money: the condo fees a month, the taxes a year;
  - space: bedrooms (0 for a studio), a den, bathrooms (a half bath counts .5), the size in square feet, the type
    (condo, apartment, townhouse, house, room, basement);
  - when: available from (to rent), possession (to buy);
  - features: each one the site names, yes or no (laundry, parking, pets and the rest);
  - who lists: owner, agent or manager, when the site says. An MLS site's search says agent for all its homes; a
    site's "professional" becomes agent or manager by its words, else not said;
  - its number: the MLS or Centris number, when shown.
- **What kestrel adds:** the area it falls in (decision 5), the other sites carrying it (`alsoOn`, decision 8), its
  price over time (as a deal's `prices`), the score's why and parts, its signs, why the rules set it aside, and the
  features read from its words.
- **Money is in whole dollars in the data.** The price, the rent, the condo fees and the taxes are stored as the
  collector door gives them, in whole dollars, as a deal's `price` is today. The kind engine counts money in cents.
  One function, `homeRow` (`src/shared/homes.ts`), builds the engine's row and turns dollars into cents there, and
  nowhere else. The card, the pills and Ask's lines all format from that row, so a rent of 2150 reads `$2,150`.

The card is the Searches card, spelled as the job and deal cards spell it:

- **Top line:** the stage chip, or "Left out · <reason>"; the deal and the area, muted ("To rent · Hiveton North"); at
  the right end, the site and "+2" when two more carry it; the ★.
- **The title and the figure:** the site's own title, a link to the listing. Beside it, the `.money` pill: `$2,150/mo`
  to rent; to buy, the price with the condo fees, `$549k + $612/mo`. With no fees given, `$549k` alone, and the facts
  say "fees not said".
- **The subline:** the address with its unit, or the site's place words.
- **The facts,** values bold: bedrooms and den, bathrooms, size; available from (to rent) or year built (to buy).
- **The match line:** its ring, the tier and the why. Then the price bar: where its money sits against the usual for
  its group (decision 6; the deal card's bar), with "8% under the usual for 2-bedroom apartments here, from 14 homes".
- **Signs:** a red chip for a scam sign, the sign named on the sheet; an orange chip for a buy's risk to check ("To
  check · special assessment").
- **Then** the deep check's verdict chip or its button, and the stage pills: Not for me, Not sure, Interested,
  Contacted, Gone. A swipe right is Interested; left, Not for me.

The pill takes its tone from where the price sits against the usual, as a deal's does. 70% or less of the usual is
never a great price: to rent it is a scam sign, in the poor tone; to buy it is a risk to check, in the fair tone.

The homes kind's tone is teal: the eyebrow ("Homes") and the hunt's pill in the switcher, as jobs take the accent and
deals orange.

Rejected: photos on the card (each would load from the site's servers on every view; the link opens them); a table on
a computer, as Shopping's compared list has (the job and deal hunts show cards at every width, so homes do too).

### 4. The filters

The page keeps the job and deal hunts' panel: the find box and Reset on top, then rows of pills with their counts,
each row folding on its own and the whole panel too. A filter's All comes first.

| Row         | Pills                                                                                                                                                         | From       |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- |
| Stage       | All, To review, ★ Priority, Interested, Not sure, Contacted, Visit booked, Applied or offered, Turned down, Gone, Not real, Not for me, Left out by the rules | every hunt |
| Match       | Great, Good, Possible, Weak                                                                                                                                   | every hunt |
| Rent or buy | All, To rent, To buy                                                                                                                                          | the kind   |
| Area        | my areas, Not placed, Outside my areas                                                                                                                        | the kind   |
| Bedrooms    | "at least" steps (1+, 2+, 3+), from the homes' own values                                                                                                     | the kind   |
| Rent        | up to three "up to" steps a month, from the rents                                                                                                             | the kind   |
| Price       | up to three "up to" steps, from the asking prices                                                                                                             | the kind   |
| Fees        | "up to" steps a month, from the condo fees                                                                                                                    | the kind   |
| Has         | each feature said yes (laundry in the unit, parking, pets allowed…); several picks at once, all required                                                      | the kind   |
| Who lists   | Owner, Agent, Manager, Not said                                                                                                                               | the kind   |
| Site        | each site, a home counted under every site that carries it                                                                                                    | the kind   |
| Order       | Best matches first, Newest first, Lowest price first                                                                                                          | every hunt |

- The page opens on To review (every new home the rules keep, whatever its match), best first, with All for rent or
  buy. To review is a deal hunt's, not a job hunt's: with my details still to come, no home would be Good, and a
  job's To review would stay empty.
- A row with nothing to choose hides. Rent hides when no rental is in view, Price and Fees when no sale is, Area until
  my areas are set.
- A money filter never keeps a home without that money: a rent step drops every sale, as Ask's pay rule drops a job
  with no stated pay.
- Lowest price first lists the rentals before the sales when both show: a month's rent is always the smaller number.
- Not applied yet stays a job hunt's switch: no home has an application.

How it runs:

- The rows every hunt shares (stage, match, the find box) stay in SQL, as now.
- The kind's rows run through `filterRows`, `pillCounts` and `numberSteps` from `src/shared/kinds.ts`, on the server.
- Each side narrows the other. The kind's filters run over the hunt's rows. They hand SQL the ids that pass, as one
  JSON list (`id IN (SELECT value FROM json_each(?))`). SQL hands the kind's counts the rows its own filters pass.
- So every pill counts under all the other filters. All of them apply before the page's limit: a starred home past
  the first page still shows under Priority.
- A homes hunt should hold a few thousand homes at most, so the pass in memory should cost milliseconds. Step H7's
  test times it on 5,000 made-up homes.
- List reads leave out the listing's words (`json_remove(data, '$.excerpt')`). The sheet reads them.
- The kind reads the one money field as Rent or as Price by the home's deal.

What the kind engine already has, as E1 built it: `atLeast` and `atMost` steps, with "up to" steps where lower is
better; a list field (the sites) whose `is` pill counts a row under each of its values; flags; money in cents.

What it lacks, added in step H7: **several picks in one row.** Today `Picked` holds one value per filter. The Has row
and Ask's `has` need several, all required. H7 adds a `several` mark on an `is` filter: its pick is a list, and a row
passes when it holds every picked value. Each pill counts the rows that pass the other rows, the picks already made
and that pill. H7 adds this to `kinds.ts` and its tests, after E1 merges. It is an addition: nothing Shopping uses
changes, and Shopping's own tests must pass unchanged.

Rejected: one flag row per feature, made from the features present (a dozen rows, each folding on its own, where one
row with several picks reads better); one Price row whose meaning changes with Rent or buy (a pill that means two
things); a Studio pill (a studio
is 0 bedrooms, and the score and the sort find them); the kind's filters translated into SQL (a second engine with the
same meaning to keep in step); every filter in memory (the stage rules would be written twice, in SQL and in code);
Shopping's kind list on this page (its own filter card and table would make the homes hunt the odd one out).

### 5. The rules, as data

A homes hunt's rules live in one file in the data folder, `searches/hunts/<key>.json`: the hunt file. Its searches
name it (`hunt: "<key>"`), and the hunt's name and About come from it. One zod shape reads it tolerantly: a lone
string where a list belongs still reads, and an unreadable part falls back to "not set", so an edit never breaks the
list.

The file holds:

- `name`, `kind` (`homes`) and `about`.
- `rent`: the budget and the stretch, a month; `staleDays` (a rental posted longer ago is set aside).
- `buy`: the budget and the stretch; the most in condo fees a month.
- `beds` (the fewest and the most), `baths` (the fewest), `size` (the smallest, in square feet), `types` (the types I
  take).
- `areas`: each a name, its postal code starts (the first characters of a postal code) and its place words
  (neighbourhoods, streets, buildings). `onlyAreas`: a home placed outside every area by its postal code is set aside;
  otherwise it only scores lower.
- `must`, `nice`, `never`: must-haves, nice-to-haves and deal-breakers, each a feature's name or plain words.
- `moveIn`: the window a home must be free in.
- `freshDays`: how long a home counts as fresh.
- `weights`: each part's points (decision 6). The defaults are in code; the weights are scaled back to 100.

Every block is optional. A part with no rule set scores half: it can't lift a home, and it can't sink one. So a hunt
with no details ranks by what it can read, has no Great home and sends no ping until I fill it in.

A place, matched with no map service:

- a postal code that starts with one of an area's starts places a home in it;
- else one of the area's place words in its address, its place words or its title;
- with neither, it is "not placed": it stays in view, scores lower, and its card says so;
- only a postal code proves a home is outside.

Saving the hunt file rescores every home the hunt holds, as saving a spec rescores its search.

Why one file: each fact is stored once (PLAN, "One source of truth"). A homes hunt spans several sites, each its own
search, and rules copied into every search's file would drift. The loader is generic: a deal hunt could take a hunt
file later. Job specs keep their own scoring, and deal searches theirs.

Rejected: a cloud map or geocoding service (not without my yes); boxes of coordinates per area against the sites' map
points (later, if names and postal starts leave too many homes unplaced); the rules copied into each search; the money
in the reasons ("$80 under the budget" would put my budget on the page Ask reads: the reasons say "under the budget",
never by how much).

### 6. How a home scores

Plain code scores a home, as it scores a job: generic, and tested on made-up homes. Each part gives points and a plain
reason. The score is their sum, 0 to 100, and the tiers are the job hunt's: Great 80 and up, Good 60 to 79, Possible
40 to 59, Weak under 40.

| Part          | Points | Full                                                                      | Less                                      |
| ------------- | ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| Money         | 30     | at or under the budget (to buy: the price 22, the fees under their cap 8) | toward the stretch, less; not given, half |
| Place         | 20     | in one of my areas                                                        | not placed, 8; placed outside, 0          |
| Space         | 20     | bedrooms in range 10, the size at least the smallest 6, bathrooms 4       | each not given, half; under, 0            |
| Must-haves    | 15     | each one said yes                                                         | not said, half                            |
| Nice-to-haves | 10     | each one said yes                                                         |                                           |
| Fresh         | 5      | posted within `freshDays`                                                 | within four times that, 2                 |

- **Features:** read from the site's own fields first, then from the words, in English and French, the "no" words
  first ("no pets", "animaux non admis"). A must-have is yes, no or not said. Not said isn't no: the home stays in view
  at half the points, and the why names what wasn't said.
- **The why,** on the card, as a job's: the reasons that moved the score, the strongest first ("in Hiveton North;
  under the budget; laundry in the unit; parking not said"). No reason holds a number from my rules.
- **Set aside** (Left out, until I mark it): over the stretch; fewer bedrooms than the fewest; a type I don't take; a
  deal-breaker named; a must-have said no; outside my areas by postal code, with `onlyAreas` on; free only after my
  window; a rental older than `staleDays`; two scam signs or more; a score under 40.
- **A scam sign caps the score at Good** (79), so such a home never enters Priority or a ping by itself.
- **The usual price:** the median of the hunt's homes in one group, seen in the last 90 days. A group is one deal,
  one type family, one bedroom count and one area. The type families are three: a room or a basement; an apartment or
  a condo; a townhouse or a house. A room is never judged against apartments, and a cheap area never against a dear
  one.
  - The card's bar needs five homes in the group. With fewer, the card has no bar.
  - The far-under sign needs ten. With fewer, no sign: a thin group can't call a home a scam.
  - A home that isn't placed has no group, so no bar and no price sign.
- **The usual moves with every run.** The card's bar is computed when the page reads it, as a deal's is (`dealOf`).
  The price sign caps the score, so it must be stored: each run ends by rescoring the whole hunt (`rescoreHunt`),
  and saving the hunt file does the same.

Rejected: a model read of every new home (it costs the local model's time; the rules, the signs and a deep check on
tap stand in, as they do for deals, 2026-11-23); the price per square foot as the usual (sizes are often missing,
bedrooms almost never); a fallback to the whole hunt when a group is thin (it compares a cheap area with a dear one,
and a room with an apartment, and calls the cheap one a scam).

### 7. Scam signs, and a buy's risks

Plain code looks for the common warning signs before any model reads the home ([Kijiji's scam page][kj-scams] and the
research's other sources). Its words, in English and French, live in a data file in the repo,
`src/domain/search/homes-words.json`: language and public rules, with no site and nothing of mine.

To rent, a scam sign is:

- a rent far under the usual of its group: 70% of it or less, from ten homes at least (decision 6);
- money before a visit: a deposit or a "hold" asked first;
- payment that can't be traced: a wire, crypto, gift cards;
- a landlord away: abroad, keys by mail, no visit possible;
- a SIN, an ID or bank details asked in the ad, or a "credit check" link;
- the same home for sale: an owner's or an unnamed lister's rental whose address and unit an agent lists for sale in
  the hunt (one building alone isn't a match), or whose own words say it is for sale;
- one home at two prices 15% or more apart, on two sites;
- a deposit asked in Quebec (a postal code starting with G, H or J), where security and key deposits are banned
  ([Kijiji's rules][kj-rules]).

To buy, the risks to check, rather than scams:

- a price far under the usual of its group: 70% of it or less, from ten homes at least;
- a special assessment named;
- "as is" or "power of sale";
- leasehold, co-op or an assignment;
- water damage, a lawsuit or litigation named;
- condo fees far under its group's per square foot: 60% or less, from ten at least. Thin fees can mean a thin reserve
  fund.

One scam sign is a red chip, and the score is capped at Good. Two or more set the home aside. The deep check makes the
call (decision 9).

**Most signs need the listing's words, and an email rarely carries them.** An alert email is a short list: a link
per home, with a few fields beside it. What each site's email holds is not checked yet (the appendix; step H14
measures it). So, for a home known only from its email:

- Only the signs built from fields can fire: the rent far under the usual, the same unit for sale, two prices on two
  sites. Each needs the address, the unit or the bedrooms, which an email may lack.
- The word signs stay silent: money before a visit, untraceable payment, a landlord away, ID asked, a deposit in
  Quebec. So do the buy risks: a special assessment, power of sale, leasehold, a lawsuit.
- The Money score is thin to buy when the email gives no fees, and Space is thin without a size.
- The card says "read from its email only", and the sheet names the signs that couldn't be looked for.

These wake up only where kestrel reads the listing's page: in a run (step H13) or in a deep check (decision 9), and
only on a site where I say yes. Until then the scam guard is mostly the deep check on my tap, working from the email
and the web.

Rejected: matching photos (a perceptual hash needs a new package and every photo downloaded; a reverse image search
is a cloud service); keeping landlords' phone numbers to match homes (other people's numbers, and the biggest site
hides them behind a reCAPTCHA); "for sale" alone as a sign (agents list many condos for sale and for lease at once,
so only an owner's or an unnamed lister's rental counts).

### 8. One home on several sites

One home is one listing, wherever it shows.

- **Its key:** the deal, the address and the unit, normalized: case, accents, street types and directions in English
  and French, and the two ways to write a unit ("Unit 1204, 55 Hive Road" and "1204-55 Hive Rd" are one home). The
  same unit to rent and to buy is two listings, linked by the scam sign above.
- **The merge:** a home that another search of the hunt finds lands on the listing already there. Its site joins
  `alsoOn` with its link, its price and when it was seen. The card says "+2"; the sheet lists each site with its price
  and link; the Site filter counts the home under each.
- **Its number:** the MLS or Centris number is a fact on the sheet, never a key on its own. A board reuses a number
  when a home is listed again, so a shared number with another address doesn't merge.
- **No address:** its link is its identity, as a deal's, and it merges only by its link. So does a condo or an
  apartment whose unit isn't given: two units of one building must never become one. Only a house or a townhouse is
  keyed by its address alone.
- **An address learned later** (step H13): when a page read gives a home the key another listing of the hunt holds,
  the two become one. The older one stays, with both sites, a star from either, both notes, and the stage I set last.
- **Deleted:** its tombstone holds its key and every site's link, read across the hunt's searches, so no site's copy
  lands it again.

Today a run's lookup stays inside its own search (`keepItems`). For homes it reads the whole hunt: the key, or a link
among the hunt's listings and their `alsoOn`. The `UNIQUE (search, dedupe)` holds, since a merged copy only adds to
`alsoOn`.

Rejected: fuzzy matching on words, price and bedrooms (two units of one building read alike, and a wrong merge hides a
home, while a missed merge only shows it twice); photos and phone numbers (decision 7).

### 9. The deep check

On my tap, as for jobs and deals: the local model reads the home, searches the web, and writes what to know. One check
runs at a time, each step in Live.

- **What it reads:** the home's facts and the words the run kept.
- **A page only on my yes.** A home known only from an email has no words. The check opens its page once, through the
  model door's page reader, only where the site's setting says yes to page reads. Elsewhere it works from the email's
  facts, and the report says so: "read from its email only; the site's pages are off".
- **One gate for every page read.** The gate sits in the page reader itself (`fetchPage`, `src/ai/web.ts`). A link on
  a site whose setting is off is refused there, for the deep check, for Ask, and for a web result that lands on such a
  site. Every site a homes search reads starts off, until its setting says yes.
- **Today's reads stay as they are.** The deal and job deep checks already open their listing's page this way
  (`src/domain/search/deep.ts`). Their sites get no setting from this plan, so nothing they do changes. Whether they
  too move behind a per-site yes is v1.0's question.
- **Never the agent's Chrome, never a sign-in.** When a site refuses the plain reader, the report works from the
  email's facts and says so.
- **What it searches:** the listing's street address without its unit, or the building's name, and never my rules.
  The address with "for rent" and "for sale" finds a clone elsewhere. The building with "special assessment",
  "reserve fund" or "lawsuit" finds its news. At most four searches a check.
- **What the searches tell.** They go through the model door's search, which asks DuckDuckGo. Nothing from the hunt
  file goes out. But a run of checks sends addresses in the areas I look at, so the search service can tell where I'm
  looking. A check runs only on my tap, so I choose each one.
- **To rent:** is it real (the code's signs and its own reading, each with where it saw it); the costs (the rent,
  what's included, what's extra, any deposit and whether the province allows it); what to ask; what to check at the
  visit. Its verdict: visit, ask first, skip, or scam.
- **To buy:** the condo fees and what they cover, against similar condos here; special assessments, said or not; the
  reserve fund, said or not; the rules (pets, rentals, short stays); the papers to ask for; red flags, questions, what
  to check at the visit. Its verdict: visit, ask first (the papers), skip, or scam.
- **The papers by province.** Code names them from the province its postal code, or its area's, gives. Ontario: the
  status certificate. BC: Form B, with the depreciation report. Alberta: the estoppel certificate. Quebec: the
  declaration of co-ownership, the syndicate's minutes and financial statements, and the contingency fund study; the
  syndicate's certificate on the building's condition only once its rule is in force, which isn't confirmed.
  Elsewhere: the financial statements and the reserve fund study. Each province's list sits in
  `homes-words.json` with its source and the date it was checked, and step H11's evals carry them.
- **What it can't know:** a listing rarely states special assessments or the reserve fund's health; the papers do. The
  report says "not said" instead of guessing.
- **A scam becomes Fake.** A scam verdict moves a home still To review, Interested or Not sure to Not real (`fake`),
  with a line in Activity. The move isn't my mark: the sheet says why, with the stages one tap away. A home I marked
  Contacted or later stays where it is, and the verdict shows on its card.

The report's shape, `HomeReport` (`src/ai/deep/shape.ts`), is read tolerantly, as the other two are: `verdict`,
`headline`, `signs` (each with where it was seen), `costs`, `fees`, `assessments`, `reserve`, `rules`, `papers`,
`questions`, `visit` and `sources`. Its prompt (`homePrompt`, `src/ai/deep/prompt.ts`) carries the code's signs, the
papers for the province and the usual price; the prompt's version goes up.

Rejected: a model read of every new home (decision 6); the check reading a page in the agent's Chrome (a paced site's
pages are opened only by runs); the check opening any listing page through the plain reader (every site's terms ban
automated reading, and the yes is mine, site by site); the unit in a web search (the street and the building find the
same clones and news).

### 10. Ask

Ask works as it does on the job and deal hunts, with homes' fields.

- **Each home's line** for the model: its id, title, deal, money (`$2,150/mo`, or `$549k + $612/mo`), bedrooms,
  bathrooms, size, area, the features said yes, its signs, its match and its stage.
- **Find and a rule use the page's own filters.** Their fields are the kind's: `words`, `deal`, `rentAtMost` and
  `rentAtLeast`, `priceAtMost` and `priceAtLeast`, `feesAtMost`, `bedsAtLeast` and `bedsAtMost`, `sizeAtLeast`,
  `area`, `has` (features said yes), `lister`, `stage` and `match`. kestrel resolves them through the page's own
  filtering (decision 4), so the model finds what my pills would. A field that isn't a home's (`payAtLeast`, `remote`)
  fails the card with why, as an unknown field does now.
- **A rule's card** says it in words, from the kind's labels ("Mark 12 homes with fewer than 2 bedrooms as Not for
  me"). It leaves out the homes I starred, those I contacted or later, and those the rules set aside, unless its stage
  names them. Undo takes it back.
- **My rules stay off its page.** The page holds the homes, their why (with no number from my rules) and the counts,
  never the hunt file. Ask has the web, and kestrel's searches take their words from a listing or my question, never
  from a profile (`src/ai/web.ts`).
- **Its page reads pass the same gate** as the deep check's (decision 9): a listing page on a site whose setting is
  off is refused, and Ask says it read the home from what the run kept.
- **Starters:** "Which ones look like scams?", "Which should I visit first?", "Find the ones with parking and laundry
  in the unit".

### 11. Pings

A hunt that is On pings once per round for its new great homes: the best in the title ("Great home: to rent · 2
bedrooms · $2,150/mo · Hiveton North · Kijiji"), three at most under it, and a tap opens it. Quiet and Paused say
nothing. A home with a scam sign is Good at most, so it never pings. The channel is `alerts.topics.homes`, else the
deals channel, as Shopping falls back; its topic is read at send time and never printed.

A ping is only as fast as its source. RentFaster mails each new match. Kijiji mails once a day. A search page read in
the agent's Chrome is the fastest, on my yes per site. Reading the alerts' mailbox costs the sites nothing, so an
alert search can read it every 30 minutes, and a ping follows its email within the half hour. All of this waits for
my yes to reading the mailbox ("What needs my mail or a login").

"One per round" is v1.0's ping rule (its part 5). If v1.0 hasn't built it when homes get there, step H12 builds it
once, for every kind.

## The sources

What the research found, read on 2026-11-25. No site offers a consumer API or an open feed: Kijiji's and Craigslist's
feeds are gone, and Facebook stopped taking partner feeds in 2021. Every site checked bans automated collection in its
terms. Canadian courts have enforced such terms (Century 21 v. Zoocasa, BC, 2011), and a 2019 Federal Court order, on
consent, declared that scraping MLS data and getting around its access controls broke the Copyright Act ([Shift
Law][shiftlaw]; [Canadian Lawyer][fc-2019]). Each site's own alert email is the one surface it offers for this use: it
stays within the terms, never meets a bot check, and doesn't break when a page changes.

The table of every source, with its access, terms, login, fields and fragility, is in the appendix. In short:

- Kijiji, Realtor.ca, Centris, DuProprio, RentFaster, Zumper and Craigslist send alert emails. All but Kijiji need an
  account of mine for them.
- Rentals.ca's alerts are unconfirmed. liv.rent has none documented. Facebook Marketplace has none confirmed and needs
  my login to read at all.
- HouseSigma, Condos.ca, Zealty and REW show sold prices only after sign-in: by hand only.
- What each site's alert email carries is not checked. The fields the research lists are the page's. Step H14
  measures each email before that site's place in the order is fixed.

### Where to start, and why

The market has three inventories, each with its own pipes: owners post on classifieds; agents list on the MLS;
property managers send each unit to twenty sites at once. One channel per inventory covers most homes, and the copies
between them fall out on the address and the unit (decision 8).

The order differs by deal: owners lead the rentals, agents lead the sales.

**To rent:**

1. **Kijiji** (owners; every province). It is the biggest owners' pool, and its alert needs no account, only an email
   address. Its scam risk is the highest, so the signs and the deep check matter most here. v1.0 ports the chair
   hunt's Kijiji reader, so reading its pages starts from that reader if I say yes.
2. **The managers' pipe:** RentFaster where it is strong, Rentals.ca elsewhere. RentFaster mails each new match, the
   fastest of the alerts. Rentals.ca's alerts aren't confirmed: I check in the browser, and without them it waits.
3. **Agents' rentals:** Realtor.ca; in Quebec, Centris.
4. **Then,** where they add homes the first three miss: Zumper (or PadMapper, never both), Kangalou in Quebec,
   Craigslist's alert in Metro Vancouver and Victoria, and liv.rent in its six metros.
5. **Last, Facebook Marketplace:** the biggest pool of private landlords and the worst scam rate. It needs my login to
   read at all, so by default it stays my own browsing.

**To buy:**

1. **Realtor.ca;** in Quebec, Centris. Agent-listed condos come with full addresses, condo fees and MLS numbers on
   their pages, so their copies merge cleanly, and their scam risk is low. Their pages sit behind bot checks, so their
   emails are the way in.
2. **DuProprio,** in Quebec: the main private-sale channel, with no copies elsewhere.
3. **Kijiji, last:** it catches private sales outside Quebec, with no account. But it is noisy and full of copies of
   MLS listings. Its condo fees sit only in the words, and it rarely gives a unit. A copy without a unit never merges
   (decision 8), so the same sale can show twice, once from Realtor.ca and once from Kijiji.
4. **By hand:** HouseSigma (or Condos.ca; Zealty or REW in BC) for sold prices and price history, which judge an
   asking price. Never through an automated signed-in account.

Each site's place is set once step H14 has measured what its email carries.

### What needs my mail or a login, plainly

- **Every alert source needs kestrel to read a mailbox. That waits for my yes** ("What waits for me", 1). Without it,
  the only sources are pages, on my yes site by site, after the agent's Chrome is kestrel's alone (the order). With
  neither yes, the real hunt has no source, and the hunt stays on made-up data.
- **The gmail command is the old setup's.** It lives in my tools folder, outside kestrel. It reads every Gmail account
  I gave it, with app passwords in the Keychain, and it can write drafts. The old hunts call it. kestrel calling it
  would be a reach outside the repo, against "kestrel must own what it runs". So kestrel never calls it.
- **kestrel's own alert reader instead.** A collector in my data folder, owned by kestrel:
  - It reads one mailbox, the one I name. Best is an address of its own that only the alerts go to, so kestrel never
    opens my personal mail.
  - It is read-only: it opens the folder read-only, never marks an email read, never writes, sends or deletes.
  - It searches by sender and since its last read, then reads each alert email's links.
  - It is a small port of the gmail command's read-only search and read, in Python's own mail libraries, with no
    package.
  - Its app password is a Keychain item of its own, read by the reader itself and never printed or logged.
  - It keeps each email's homes (the link, the price, the place, the bedrooms) and nothing else. No email's words
    reach a model or a log: the run counts emails and homes.
- **v1.0 has the same gap.** Its plan reads the job-alert emails through the gmail command (its part 2). This reader
  would serve the job alerts too. v1.0's text changes when I say yes, not in this plan.
- **Accounts only I can make:** Realtor.ca, Centris, DuProprio, Zumper and Craigslist need one for their alerts, and
  RentFaster most likely does too. kestrel never signs in to them: it only reads the emails they send. Kijiji's alert
  needs only the email address.
- **A login to read at all:** Facebook Marketplace. Only on my yes, in the agent's Chrome signed in to my own account,
  at the waits its site settings set (v1.0's part 1), once the old setup no longer drives that Chrome. Captchas and
  account limits are reported there.
- **Sold prices behind a sign-in:** HouseSigma, Condos.ca, Zealty and REW. By hand only.

### How kestrel reads them

The rules, in the engine and with no site names:

1. **Alert emails first,** on my yes to the mailbox. Each alert search names its mail query (who sends, the alert's
   name), and its deal when its emails don't say.
2. **An empty read is told apart.** "No new email" is fine: the run lands without a problem. "An email gave no home"
   (a changed layout) and "no email for three days" (the alert stopped) are problems, in plain words. Today a run that
   finds nothing is always a problem ("the page answered empty"). So the collector door gains an object answer: the
   items, a calm word when nothing new is fine (`{"items": [], "quiet": "no new email"}`), or a problem beside the
   items it did find.
3. **A new home's own page is read once,** only where its site's setting says yes: in the agent's Chrome, at the
   site's pace, capped per run (step H13). Its facts and words come from it. A home whose page isn't read scores on
   what its email says.
4. **Search pages only where no alert works,** on my yes per site, the way the chair hunt reads Kijiji and
   Marketplace.
5. **The agent's Chrome only once it is kestrel's alone.** The old setup runs the chair hunt again (2026-11-25), in
   the same Chrome. kestrel driving it at the same time would break "one search tab" (v1.0) and the rule that the
   side-by-side week never double-drives the browser (2026-11-21). kestrel's waits can't see the old engine's either.
   So every read in the agent's Chrome waits until no old hunt drives it.
6. **A check stops the site's run.** A login wall, a captcha or a bot check stops it with one ping (v1.0's login
   pause). Nothing gets around a check: no private API, no proxy, no disguised browser.
7. **Outside runs, a page only through the gate.** The deep check and Ask read what the run kept. They open a
   listing's page once, through the model door's plain reader, only where its site's setting says yes (decision 9).

## After the build

A reviewer reads the whole version against this plan, and a fixer applies what it finds, one commit each. The live
evals of Ask's homes cases and the deep check run on both local models, with my yes, and are kept in `docs/eval/`.
Then a deploy.

## What waits for me

None of these blocks the build: steps H1 to H12 run on made-up data, and H13 on stand-ins. The answers go in the
data folder, never in this repo.

1. **May kestrel read a mailbox for the alerts?** Every alert source depends on it. If yes:
   - which mailbox (best, an address of its own that only the alerts go to);
   - its app password, saved by me in the Keychain under the reader's own item;
   - kestrel's own read-only alert reader, not the old setup's gmail command ("What needs my mail or a login").

   If no, the only sources are pages, on my yes per site (item 4), once no old hunt drives the agent's Chrome. With
   neither, the real hunt has no source and stays on made-up data.
2. **My details,** into the hunt file: the areas (names, postal starts, place words) and whether a home outside them is
   set aside; the budget and the stretch for each deal I want; the most in condo fees; the space; the must-haves,
   nice-to-haves and deal-breakers; the move-in window.
3. **The alerts,** on a yes to item 1: set up on each site I use and sent to that mailbox, with the accounts they need.
4. **Page reads,** per site: its alert email only, or also its pages, at its pace. A yes also lets the deep check and
   Ask open that site's listing pages. Every site's terms forbid automated access. The old setup's chair hunt already
   reads Kijiji and Marketplace this way. Without page reads, most scam signs and a buy's risks stay silent (decision
   7).
5. **Facebook Marketplace:** my own browsing, or the agent's Chrome on my account.
6. **Pings for great homes:** a topic of their own, the deals channel, or Quiet.
7. **To buy, a buyer's agent:** an agent can send the MLS's own saved-search emails, the fullest feed a buyer can get.
   It means working with an agent.
8. **The live evals** of Ask's homes cases and the deep check, on both local models, before I first use them.

## Out of scope

- Paid APIs, paid scrapers and proxies, and a site's private API (Realtor.ca's, Centris's, Zumper's, HouseSigma's).
- Getting around a captcha, a login or a bot check, or disguising the agent's browser (PLAN's out of scope).
- Contacting landlords or agents, applying to rent, booking visits, making offers: the deep check says what to ask,
  and I write.
- A cloud map or geocoding service, without my yes.
- A reverse image search (a cloud service) and photo hashing (a new package).
- Mortgage and rent-or-buy maths.
- Sold prices behind a sign-in, by automation: by hand only.
- Pre-construction and assignment sales: mostly off the MLS, with sources of their own.
